# Bound-Constrained Fairing of Voxel-Derived Meshes: Lineage, Solvers, and a Recommended Practice

## TL;DR
- Your problem is the discrete **obstacle problem** (an elliptic variational inequality) dressed as geometry processing: minimize a sparse SPD thin-plate/membrane energy subject to per-vertex box ("corridor") bounds and Dirichlet pins. Because the constraints are axis-aligned, it decouples into three independent scalar bound-constrained QPs, each of which should be solved with a **specialized bound-constrained solver (projected/active-set conjugate gradient à la Moré–Toraldo, or monotone multigrid à la Kornhuber)**, never a general dense QP.
- The historical line runs **Taubin 1995 (explicit λ|μ filtering, shrinkage) → Desbrun et al. 1999 (implicit/backward-Euler fairing, unconditional stability) → Kobbelt discrete fairing & higher-order energies → constrained fairing → the obstacle-problem/box-QP formulation**, with **Gibson 1998 "Constrained Elastic Surface Nets"** being the direct geometry-processing ancestor of your corridor: keeping each node inside its originating cell is exactly a per-vertex box constraint and is precisely what prevents thin-member collapse.
- Your thinning was inevitable: plain Taubin/Laplacian smoothing cannot enforce occupancy, so opposite walls of a thin member diffuse toward each other. The corridor fixes this only if it is **thickness-adaptive / signed-distance-asymmetric / per-voxel** rather than a fixed radial clamp (a fixed radius r pinches members thinner than 2r). Recommended solver stack: **projected-gradient + CG on the free set (Moré–Toraldo GPCG / Dostál proportioning), warm-started coarse-to-fine, with monotone multigrid (Kornhuber) if you need to scale to millions of vertices**; OSQP is a robust fallback but is a general QP and leaves the box structure on the table.

## Key Findings

1. **The formulation is the obstacle problem.** Minimizing a Dirichlet/thin-plate energy subject to a pointwise inequality (lo ≤ x ≤ hi) is the classical obstacle problem, an elliptic variational inequality whose continuous theory was founded by **Lions & Stampacchia, "Variational inequalities," Comm. Pure Appl. Math. 20 (1967), 493–519**. Its finite-element discretization is exactly a **bound-constrained convex QP with SPD Hessian** — identical in structure to your Q = L_sᵀM⁻¹L_s system. This identification is the single most useful fact in the report: it gives you a 50-year-old, well-understood solver literature.

2. **The geometry-processing lineage is clear and your corridor has a named ancestor.** Taubin (1995) reframed fairing as low-pass filtering and introduced the λ|μ two-pass non-shrinking filter; Desbrun, Meyer, Schröder & Barr (1999) made it implicit (backward Euler) for unconditional stability; Kobbelt and Schneider & Kobbelt formalized higher-order (membrane k=1, thin-plate k=2) discrete energies; and **Gibson's Constrained Elastic Surface Nets (MICCAI 1998)** introduced the per-cell containment constraint that is mathematically your box corridor.

3. **With only box constraints and an SPD Hessian, specialized solvers dominate general QP.** Projected Gauss–Seidel/SOR (classic obstacle solver), gradient-projection + CG (Moré–Toraldo 1991, GPCG), proportioning (Dostál 1997), primal-dual active set = semismooth Newton (Hintermüller–Ito–Kunisch 2002), and monotone multigrid (Kornhuber 1994/1996) all exploit the bound structure. General QP solvers (OSQP, qpOASES) ignore it and are slower per unknown, though OSQP is very robust and trivially warm-started.

## Details

### 1. The discrete operators: what Q = L_sᵀM⁻¹L_s actually is

The cotangent weight formula for the discrete Laplace–Beltrami operator is classically attributed in graphics to **Pinkall & Polthier, "Computing Discrete Minimal Surfaces and Their Conjugates," Experimental Mathematics 2(1) (1993), 15–36**, who obtained it by minimizing Dirichlet energy over a triangulation. The formula has an earlier finite-element/electrical-network origin in **R. H. MacNeal's 1949 Caltech PhD thesis** (with the cotan construction credited to Stanley Frankel) and **R. J. Duffin, "Distributed and lumped networks," J. Math. Mech. 8 (1959), 793–826**. (Keenan Crane's note "The n-dimensional cotangent formula" documents this attribution chain.)

The **mixed Voronoi mass matrix** — your M — is standardized by **Meyer, Desbrun, Schröder & Barr, "Discrete Differential-Geometry Operators for Triangulated 2-Manifolds," Visualization and Mathematics III, Springer 2003 (VisMath '02), pp. 35–57**. They define the cotangent Laplace–Beltrami operator as K(xᵢ) = (1/2·A_Mixed) Σ_{j∈N₁(i)} (cot αᵢⱼ + cot βᵢⱼ)(xᵢ − xⱼ), where αᵢⱼ, βᵢⱼ are the two angles opposite edge (xᵢ,xⱼ), and A_Mixed is assembled per-triangle (Voronoi region for non-obtuse triangles; area/2 if the angle at xᵢ is obtuse, area/4 otherwise). They note this "is equivalent to a local adjustment of the diagonal mass matrix in a finite element framework in order to ensure a correct evaluation."

The **higher-order energy** structure E_k = ∫(Δ^{k/2} f)² (k=1 membrane/stretching, k=2 thin-plate/bending) and its discretization as a symmetric SPD system is given in **Botsch & Sorkine, "On Linear Variational Surface Deformation Methods," IEEE TVCG 14(1) (2008), 213–230**. They write L = M⁻¹L_s with M the diagonal Voronoi mass matrix (M_ii = A_i) and L_s the symmetric cotangent matrix; the thin-plate Euler–Lagrange system, premultiplied by M, becomes the **symmetric positive-(semi)definite system (−k_s L_s + k_b L_s M⁻¹ L_s) d = M b** — i.e., your bi-Laplacian term is **L_s M⁻¹ L_s**, exactly the Q you describe. The order k of derivatives in the energy ((−1)ᵏ Δ_S^k d = 0) sets the resulting continuity C^{k−1}: k=1 gives the membrane (−Δp=0, C⁰), k=2 the thin-plate (Δ²p=0, C¹). This is the textbook treatment in **Botsch, Kobbelt, Pauly, Alliez & Lévy, "Polygon Mesh Processing" (AK Peters, 2010)**, whose smoothing/fairing chapter casts fairing as constrained energy minimization with boundary constraints. (Corroborated by Jacobson & Stein, "Natural Boundary Conditions for Smoothing in Geometry Processing," which discretizes the H² thin-plate energy with the same lumped-mass L^T M⁻¹ L pattern, and by libigl conventions.)

### 2. Geometry-processing lineage of fair surface design

**Taubin (1995)** — Gabriel Taubin, "A Signal Processing Approach to Fair Surface Design," SIGGRAPH '95, pp. 351–358. Core idea: generalize Fourier analysis to mesh signals; fairing = low-pass filtering with the umbrella/graph Laplacian. The naive Laplacian (Gaussian) smoother shrinks the mesh; Taubin's fix is the **two-pass λ|μ filter** R = (I+μL)(I+λL) with a positive shrinking step λ followed by a negative anti-shrinking step μ (|μ| slightly larger than λ), approximating an ideal low-pass response. It is **explicit (forward) relaxation** — O(n) per iteration, linear time and space — and that is exactly why it cannot enforce an occupancy constraint: each pass is an unconstrained local average. This is the method you had, and the **shrinkage/thinning you observed is its documented, intrinsic failure mode**.

**Desbrun, Meyer, Schröder & Barr (1999)** — "Implicit Fairing of Irregular Meshes using Diffusion and Curvature Flow," SIGGRAPH '99, pp. 317–324. The pivotal step: solve the diffusion/fairing equation with **implicit (backward Euler)** integration, (I − λ·dt·L) x^{n+1} = x^n, which is **unconditionally stable** and allows arbitrarily large time steps (the explicit scheme has a CFL-type step restriction). They introduced scale-dependent umbrella operators, the curvature-flow operator, exact volume preservation, and hard/soft positional constraints. The implicit solve is a **sparse linear system**, solved by preconditioned CG or sparse Cholesky. This is the conceptual bridge from filtering to **solving an equilibrium system** — and the equilibrium-with-bounds version is your QP.

**Kobbelt and discrete fairing** — Leif Kobbelt, "Discrete Fairing," Proc. 7th IMA Conf. on the Mathematics of Surfaces (1997), 101–131; and Kobbelt, Campagna, Vorsatz & Seidel, "Interactive Multiresolution Modeling on Arbitrary Meshes," SIGGRAPH '98, 105–114. These define fairing as the **constrained minimization of discrete energy functionals** (membrane k=1, thin-plate k=2, and higher via iterated Laplacians) and embed it in a multiresolution decomposition for fast, detail-preserving editing. Schneider & Kobbelt refine the bi-Laplacian energy and intrinsic-PDE fairing ("Geometric fairing of irregular meshes for free-form surface design," CAGD 18(4) (2001), 359–379; "Improved bi-Laplacian mesh fairing," Mathematical Methods for Curves and Surfaces, 2001; "Mesh Fairing based on an Intrinsic PDE Approach," Computer-Aided Design 33(11) (2001), 767–777).

**Variational editing as sparse SPD solves** — Sorkine, Cohen-Or, Lipman, Alexa, Rössl & Seidel, "Laplacian Surface Editing," SGP 2004; Lipman et al. "Differential coordinates"/"Laplacian Framework for Interactive Mesh Editing" (2004/2005); Yu et al. Poisson mesh editing (2004). All reduce to **sparse least-squares / SPD linear systems** with positional (Dirichlet) constraints, solved interactively via a precomputed Cholesky factorization of the coefficient matrix. Your equality "pins" are exactly these Dirichlet constraints; the corridor adds the inequality layer they lack.

**Constrained fairing, explicitly** — Liu et al., "Constrained Fairing for Meshes" (Computer Graphics Forum), present "a novel fairing algorithm for the removal of noise from uniform triangular meshes without shrinkage and serious distortion. The key feature of this algorithm is to keep all triangle centers invariant at each smoothing step by including some constraints in the energy minimization functional," minimized iteratively — an early explicit instance of constrained (rather than free) energy minimization for meshes, with membrane and thin-plate energy terms.

### 3. The corridor's true ancestor: Constrained Elastic Surface Nets

**Sarah F. F. Gibson, "Constrained Elastic Surface Nets: Generating Smooth Surfaces from Binary Segmented Data," MICCAI 1998 (LNCS 1496), pp. 888–898** (extended version: MERL Tech. Rep. TR99-24, April 1999). This is the direct lineage of your corridor. A net of linked surface nodes (one per surface cell of the binary volume) is relaxed to reduce surface energy; verbatim: "The positions of the nodes are adjusted iteratively to reduce energy in the surface net while satisfying the constraint that each element in the surface net must remain within its original surface cube. This constraint ensures that fine detail such as cracks and thin protrusions that are present in the binary data are maintained." A related Gibson patent describes the constraint as one that "favors the original segmentation over smoothness and forces the surface model to retain thin structures and cracks." In other words, **the per-cell containment constraint is exactly a per-vertex axis-aligned box, and its explicit purpose is preventing thin-feature collapse**, the very failure you hit. The relaxation in the original is a projected/clamped iteration (move to reduce curvature, then clamp back into the cell) — a **projected Jacobi/Gauss–Seidel step on the box-constrained energy**.

This thread continues to the present: **Frisken, "SurfaceNets for Multi-Label Segmentations with Preservation of Sharp Boundaries," J. Computer Graphics Techniques 11(1) (2022), 34–54** extends 3D SurfaceNets to multi-material labels and "preserves topology and sharp boundaries between materials, guarantees a user-specified accuracy," achieved by keeping each node within a bounded distance of its originating cell during fairing. This underlies the VTK `vtkSurfaceNets3D` filter (Schroeder, Tsalikis, Halle & Frisken, 2023). The dual-contouring family — **Ju, Losasso, Schaefer & Warren, "Dual Contouring of Hermite Data," SIGGRAPH 2002, ACM TOG 21(3), 339–346**, and Kobbelt, Botsch, Schwanecke & Seidel, "Feature Sensitive Surface Extraction from Volume Data," SIGGRAPH 2001 — place a vertex inside each cell by minimizing a quadratic error function (QEF), and surface vertices can be moved around inside the cell, again a per-cell box. (Note: pure dual contouring needs Hermite data — exact intersections and normals — which your pure 0/1 occupancy does not provide, so the SurfaceNets/cuberille + constrained-fairing route is the correct match for your data, not Hermite DC.)

### 4. Numerical solution of the bound-constrained sparse SPD QP

The decisive structural fact: **with only box constraints and an SPD Hessian, the problem is far easier than general QP**, and specialized solvers exploit that. General QP solvers handle arbitrary linear inequalities Ax ≤ b; you have none — only lo ≤ x ≤ hi — so the "active set" is just "which variables are clamped," and projection onto the feasible set is a trivial per-coordinate clamp. Below, methods ordered roughly by sophistication.

**(a) Projected Gauss–Seidel / Projected SOR (PGS/PSOR).** The classic obstacle-problem/LCP solver: do a Gauss–Seidel sweep, clamp each updated component into [lo_i, hi_i] immediately. Cottle, Pang & Stone, "The Linear Complementarity Problem" (1992) is the reference; convergence is linear for SPD systems. This is essentially what Gibson's surface-net relaxation does. **Core idea:** cheap, matrix-free, O(nnz) per sweep, trivially handles bounds; **robustness:** very robust, monotone energy decrease; **weakness:** linear convergence degrades as the mesh refines (iteration count grows with condition number ~ h⁻²), so it is slow to high accuracy on fine meshes. Handles **box constraints natively**.

**(b) Gradient projection + conjugate gradient (the workhorse).** **Moré & Toraldo, "On the Solution of Large Quadratic Programming Problems with Bound Constraints," SIAM J. Optim. 1 (1991), 93–113** — the GPCG algorithm. Core idea: use **gradient-projection steps to rapidly identify the active set** (which variables sit at their bounds), then run **CG on the reduced "free" subspace** (a Dirichlet system on the unclamped DOFs) to high accuracy; repeat. This is exactly the active-set structure you sketched (solve free DOFs as a Dirichlet system, clamp to bounds, update active set). The authors prove that "for strictly convex problems the algorithm converges to the solution, and that if the solution is nondegenerate, then the algorithm terminates at the solution in a finite number of steps." Their test problems are precisely yours' relatives — "the obstacle problem, the elastic-plastic torsion problem, and the journal bearing problems" — and "on a selection of these problems with dimensions ranging from 5000 to 15,000, the algorithm determines the solution in fewer than 15 iterations, and with a small number of function-gradient evaluations and Hessian-vector products per iteration." **Closely related:** Dostál's **proportioning with projections** (Z. Dostál, "Box constrained quadratic programming with proportioning and projections," SIAM J. Optim. 7(3) (1997), 871–887) and its rate-of-convergence refinements (Dostál & Schöberl, "Minimizing quadratic functions subject to bound constraints with the rate of convergence and finite termination," Comput. Optim. Appl. 30 (2005), 23–43) give a **linear convergence rate bounded by the spectral condition number of the Hessian**, can add/remove many indices per step, and are provably finite-terminating. This family is the recommended default: efficient, robust, **box-native**, and warm-startable.

**(c) Primal-dual active set = semismooth Newton.** **Hintermüller, Ito & Kunisch, "The Primal-Dual Active Set Strategy as a Semismooth Newton Method," SIAM J. Optim. 13(3) (2002), 865–888.** Core idea: predict the active set from the primal variable and the dual multiplier (via the max/complementarity function), solve the resulting equality-constrained system, and repeat; this is identical to a semismooth Newton iteration on the KKT/complementarity system. **Robustness/speed:** locally **superlinear** convergence and, in practice, "extremely efficient" for obstacle problems — often converging in a handful of iterations largely independent of mesh size, with global convergence via merit functions. Handles **box/bilateral bounds** directly. Caveat: each iteration solves a linear system on the inactive set, which for fourth-order (bi-Laplacian) problems can be ill-conditioned and benefits from preconditioning (cf. Brenner–Davis–Sung, additive Schwarz preconditioners for the clamped-Kirchhoff-plate obstacle problem).

**(d) Multigrid for obstacle problems — the scalability answer.** **Kornhuber, "Monotone multigrid methods for elliptic variational inequalities I/II," Numer. Math. 69 (1994), 167–184 and 72 (1996), 481–499.** Core idea: combine a projected (monotone) Gauss–Seidel smoother with coarse-grid corrections constrained to respect the fine-grid obstacle, yielding **monotone energy decrease and multigrid (near mesh-independent) convergence rates** even with the inequality. Predecessors: Brandt & Cryer, "Multigrid algorithms for the solution of linear complementarity problems arising from free boundary problems," SIAM J. Sci. Stat. Comput. 4 (1983), 655–684 (projected full-approximation scheme, PFAS); Hackbusch & Mittelmann (1983); Hoppe (1987). **This is what you escalate to if PGS/GPCG iteration counts become unacceptable on multi-million-vertex meshes** — it makes the corridor obstacle problem genuinely scalable (near-O(n)). Handles **box constraints** by construction.

**(e) General QP solvers (fallbacks).** **OSQP** (Stellato, Banjac, Goulart, Bemporad & Boyd, "OSQP: An Operator Splitting Solver for Quadratic Programs," Math. Program. Comput. 12(4) (2020), 637–672) is an ADMM-based first-order solver: very robust ("placing no requirements on the problem data such as positive definiteness of the objective function or linear independence of the constraint functions"), detects infeasibility, supports **factorization caching and warm starting** (it solves a quasi-definite system with the same factorization every iteration). It is excellent as a drop-in, especially across warm-started outer iterations, but it treats your bounds as generic constraints and converges only to moderate accuracy quickly. **qpOASES** (Ferreau et al., online active-set) is an active-set QP that warm-starts beautifully but, per the OSQP authors' own benchmarks, was excluded from the Maros–Mészáros test set "because it could not solve most of the problems since it is not suited for large QPs – it is based on an active-set method with dense linear algebra" (the number of active-set combinations becomes extremely large). It is fine for small/dense problems, wrong tool for a mesh-scale sparse system. Interior-point QP (e.g., the Cafieri–D'Apuzzo–Toraldo et al. potential-reduction method for sparse bound-constrained QP) is robust and polynomial but is hard to warm-start across resolution levels, which negates the coarse-to-fine advantage.

**Per-axis decoupling and warm starting.** Because both the pins and the corridor are axis-aligned, Q is shared across x/y/z and the three scalar QPs differ only in RHS/bounds — so you **factor/precondition once and solve three times**, and in a coarse-to-fine scheme you **warm-start each level from the prolongated coarse solution and re-derive the corridor after subdivision**. Active-set/semismooth-Newton and PGS warm-start by reusing the previous active set; OSQP warm-starts primal+dual iterates; multigrid is itself the coarse-to-fine vehicle.

### 5. Robustness and the thinning failure mode

Your reported thinning has a precise geometric explanation and precise fixes:

- **Why plain Taubin thins:** each λ|μ pass is an unconstrained local average; with no occupancy constraint, the two opposing walls of a thin member each move toward the member's centerline, and over iterations the member necks and collapses. The λ|μ scheme controls *volume shrinkage of the whole surface* but provides **no per-location lower bound**, so thin features are unprotected.
- **Why a fixed-radius corridor still pinches:** if you build the corridor as a symmetric band of half-width r around the input surface (clamp each vertex to within r of its start), then for a member of wall-thickness t < 2r the bands from the two opposite walls **overlap**, the feasible interval collapses (lo > hi after intersection) or admits the centerline, and the member is still pinched. The corridor must be **thinner than half the local feature size**.
- **Standard fixes:** (i) **thickness-adaptive corridor width** — shrink the allowed band where the signed-distance transform indicates a thin member (local feature size from the SDT or medial-axis distance); (ii) **per-voxel containment instead of a radial clamp** — Gibson's original: clamp each vertex to its originating cell / to the solid–void interface band, so solid voxels stay inside and void voxels stay outside *by construction* (occupancy-consistent, not radius-consistent); (iii) **signed-distance-based asymmetric bounds** — derive lo_i, hi_i per axis from the SDT so the bound on the "solid side" differs from the "void side," which both preserves occupancy and prevents opposite-wall overlap. The SDT-of-binary literature warns that distances from a pure binary are severely quantized (curvatures from a binary SDT are "severely quantized compared with" smoother embeddings), so regularizing the SDT (e.g., light Gaussian/curvature-flow smoothing, or Gibson's anti-aliased distance map) before deriving bounds improves the corridor. Note also that standard TSDF fusion "cannot simultaneously capture both sides of thin structures due to destructive averaging in the integration band," which is why per-axis/directional or per-cell bounds outperform a single radial band on thin members.
- **Manifoldness / no self-intersection:** the box constraint alone guarantees occupancy consistency but not the absence of self-intersection under large smoothing; in practice, keeping the corridor narrow (sub-voxel) plus the energy's own smoothness keeps the constrained solve well-behaved, and SurfaceNets-style methods report topology preservation as a benefit of the per-cell constraint.

## Recommendations

**Stage 1 — Formulate and decouple (do this regardless of scale).** Build Q = L_sᵀ M⁻¹ L_s with the cotangent L_s (Pinkall–Polthier / Meyer et al.) and mixed-Voronoi lumped M (Meyer et al. 2003). Use the bi-Laplacian (thin-plate, k=2) energy for fairness; fall back to membrane (k=1, xᵀL_cot x) only if you want C⁰ tangential continuity or hit conditioning trouble. Impose pins as hard equality constraints (eliminate those rows). Solve **three independent scalar box-QPs** (x/y/z) sharing Q. Derive the corridor [lo_i, hi_i] **per axis from a (regularized) signed-distance transform of the binary**, asymmetric about the solid–void interface, and **never wider than half the local feature size** — this is the single most important robustness lever.

**Stage 2 — Default solver: gradient-projection + CG on the free set.** Implement Moré–Toraldo GPCG or Dostál proportioning-with-projections: gradient-projection to fix the active (clamped) set, CG on the free Dirichlet sub-system, iterate. Precondition the free-set CG (incomplete Cholesky, or reuse the unconstrained Cholesky factor of Q). This is box-native, robust, finite-terminating, and — on the canonical obstacle problems — converges in well under ~15 iterations at moderate scale. Warm-start the active set across the three axes and across outer/subdivision iterations. **Benchmark/threshold:** if it converges in ≲30–50 iterations per axis at your target accuracy, stop here.

**Stage 3 — If iteration counts balloon on fine meshes, escalate to monotone multigrid.** If the mesh exceeds ~10⁶ vertices and GPCG iteration counts grow with resolution (the tell-tale h⁻² blow-up of any single-level method), switch the linear engine to Kornhuber monotone multigrid (projected-GS smoother + constrained coarse corrections), which restores near mesh-independent convergence. Use your cuberille resolution hierarchy as the grid hierarchy and **re-derive the corridor at each level after subdivision**.

**Stage 4 — Pragmatic fallback / prototyping.** If you want a library now, use **OSQP** per axis: assemble P=Q, the bounds as l ≤ x ≤ u (identity A), cache the factorization, and warm-start across axes and outer iterations. Accept moderate accuracy. Avoid qpOASES (not for large sparse QP) and avoid dense/general interior-point QP unless the problem is small. For a primal-dual active-set / semismooth-Newton option (Hintermüller–Ito–Kunisch), expect very fast convergence but invest in preconditioning the inactive-set solves for the bi-Laplacian.

**Thresholds that change the recommendation:** (a) if thin-member collapse persists *with* the corridor, your bounds are too wide — tighten to per-cell containment and verify occupancy after the solve, voxel by voxel; (b) if the constrained solve self-intersects, narrow the corridor and/or add the membrane term to penalize stretching; (c) if interactivity is required and the mesh is static across edits, precompute the Cholesky factor of the free-set system and warm-start the active set (interactive-rate editing à la Laplacian Surface Editing).

## Caveats
- **Source-quality notes:** the optimization claims (Moré–Toraldo, Dostál, Kornhuber, Hintermüller–Ito–Kunisch, Lions–Stampacchia, OSQP) are from primary papers/journals and are well established. The MacNeal-1949-vs-Duffin-1959 priority for the cotangent formula is a documented but minor scholarly disagreement; the safe graphics citation is Pinkall–Polthier 1993 with Meyer et al. 2003 for the mass matrix.
- **Pure occupancy vs. Hermite data:** Hermite-based dual contouring (Ju et al. 2002) is *not* directly applicable to pure 0/1 occupancy because it requires exact edge intersections and normals; the correct match for your data is the cuberille + welding + constrained-fairing (SurfaceNets) route. Where I describe DC, it is for lineage, not as a recommended extractor for your inputs.
- **Convergence-rate statements** (e.g., "fewer than 15 iterations," mesh-independent multigrid rates) are reported for the authors' specific model problems (obstacle, torsion, journal-bearing); your bi-Laplacian corridor problem is fourth-order and may need preconditioning to realize comparable counts — treat the numbers as indicative, not guaranteed.
- **Volume/feature preservation** from the box constraint guarantees occupancy consistency, not absence of self-intersection or guaranteed manifoldness under arbitrarily aggressive smoothing; validate occupancy and manifoldness after the solve.
- I was unable, within budget, to locate a single published paper that states your *exact* pipeline (cuberille + welding from pure binary → per-axis SDT corridor → per-axis bi-Laplacian box-QP). The report therefore synthesizes the established adjacent literatures (constrained surface nets + obstacle-problem QP); the synthesis and solver recommendation are my judgment, well-supported by those primary sources but not a citation of one prior identical system.
